# 2. VORP

*ADVANCED · 3m read · 2026*

**Value Over Replacement Player - How it works, why it is important, and how the WalterPicks Draft Tool can help you dominate with it.**

Nothing in life is free. This is because everything comes with an opportunity cost (what you could have gotten or done instead). In fantasy drafts, when you draft a player with your first pick, the opportunity cost is all of the other players you could have taken with that pick who are no longer available with your next pick.

With every pick, we need to consider the opportunity cost of the players and positions we are passing on. This is exactly what the Value Over Replacement Player (VORP) formula helps us with. Math is the best, right?

## What is VORP?

VORP is a way to quantify how much better a player performs compared to the typical “replacement level” player at their position. Popularized by **Keith Woolner** for Baseball Prospectus, this methodology has been adapted for fantasy football to identify market inefficiencies and optimize draft value.

Replacement level players are players likely to be available towards the end of your draft or on the waiver wire after the draft.

## How it works in practice

Let’s say **Josh Allen** is available with the 23rd overall pick. WalterPicks projects him as the overall QB1 with 22.4 points per game for 2026. **Kyler Murray**, projected as QB14 with 18 PPG, is available in round 12 of 12-team drafts (Sleeper ADP: 143). This creates a VORP of 4.4 PPG for Allen, representing the tangible advantage you are projected to gain by investing an early-round pick in the QB position versus waiting to draft a QB late.

If you instead were looking at RB with the 23rd overall pick, you could draft **Kenneth Walker** (Sleeper ADP: 20) who projects for 16.5 PPG. The replacement player here would be **Tyjae Spears** (Sleeper ADP: 148) who projects as RB42 with 6.8 PPG, giving Walker a VORP of 9.7 PPG.

VORP helps you decide ahead of time “Would you rather have Josh Allen and Tyjae Spears or Kyler Murray and Kenneth Walker?”

The data is clear: star RBs and WRs generate substantially higher VORP than their QB and TE counterparts. This positional scarcity principle explains why the opening rounds of 1QB league drafts are predominantly populated by skill position players rather than quarterbacks, regardless of raw scoring projections.

## The Key Insight

Fantasy football draft success is not about accumulating the most points as soon as possible, it is about maximizing your competitive advantage relative to the replacement-level alternatives available later in drafts.

# 3. Regression

*ADVANCED · 4m read · 2026*

The word “regress” comes with a negative connotation in the sports world. But in math, “regression” can be both positive and negative. It simply means that over time we should expect outcomes to trend towards their average.

In a fantasy football context:

- **“Positive regression” is generally good** - these are bounceback candidates. These are situations when a player is coming off a season where they significantly underperformed their career average or expected efficiency. This is good because we should expect these players to “regress” towards more positive outcomes but that is rarely fully captured in their draft cost.
- **“Negative regression” is generally bad** - these are overrated players. These are situations when a player significantly overperforms their career average or expected efficiency. We should expect these players to “regress” towards more negative outcomes and they often have inflated draft cost.

For fantasy football, it makes the most sense to focus on outlier touchdown rate outcomes from last season because touchdown rates have a high variance from year-to-year and touchdowns have a huge impact on fantasy football success.

Every season, understanding both the positive and negative touchdown regression candidates identifies major draft-day values, because the average draft position for players this draft season is heavily influenced by last year’s fantasy success, with regression often getting overlooked.

Here are just a few negative touchdown regression examples from the past few years:

- **Raheem Mostert** - 2023: 8.6% → 2024: 2.4%
- **De’Von Achane** - 2023: 7.8% → 2024: 3.0%
- **Kenneth Gainwell** - 2022: 7.6% → 2023: 2.4%
- **Mark Andrews** - 2024: 19.6% → 2025: 10.9%
- **Terry McLaurin** - 2024: 16.2% → 2025: 8.8%

Because so much of this year’s draft cost is weighted towards touchdown performance from last season, **negative regression candidates often get drafted too early, while positive regression candidates are drafted too late.**

Math helps account for this, which is why Walter’s projections have been excellent at identifying regression candidates, whether negative or positive. Last year this led to many of the successful hits from the DO NOT DRAFT list, like **Sam LaPorta and Mark Andrews** as well as some of the biggest hits from the targets list like **De’Von Achane and Puka Nacua**. # 4. Injuries + Recency Bias

*ADVANCED · 4m read · 2026*

Injuries are by far *the worst* part of fantasy football. Injuries play a major role in fantasy football every single season. People will often point to injuries as their argument for why fantasy football is “all luck,” and while injuries certainly do introduce some amount of luck into the game of fantasy football, injuries are something skilled fantasy managers can prepare for and strategize around.

We *do not* know which players will get hurt this season, but here is what we do know:

1. Fantasy managers generally *overestimate* their ability to predict injuries.
2. Every NFL player has significant injury risk, football is one of the most injury-prone sports in the world.
3. Certain positions have higher injury risk than others, but there can be a ton of year-to-year variance. I dug into the data over the last 15 years, here is the breakdown of injury rates by position:

### Running Backs
- **5.2% injury rate per game**
- **62% of RBs miss at least one game per season**
- **3.9 games missed per injury**

### Wide Receivers
- **4.5% injury rate per game**
- **50% of WRs miss at least one game per season**
- **3.2 games missed per injury**

### Tight Ends
- **4.9% injury rate per game**
- **49% of TEs miss at least one game per season**
- **2.6 games missed per injury**

### Quarterbacks
- **2.5% injury rate per game**
- **31% of QBs miss at least one game per season**
- **3.1 games missed per injury**

### NFL Position Injury Rates

|---|---:|---:|---:|

4. Backups or “handcuff” running backs benefit disproportionately to backups at other positions in the event of injuries. This is core to the thesis of the “Zero-RB” strategy we will discuss in the next section.
5. Some players are already *actively* injured and at risk to miss games this NFL season. It’s important to be up-to-date with each injury ahead of your draft and to tread very carefully with these players. The WalterPicks app updates in real-time for injury-related news.

## Recency Bias

Recency bias is a common psychological bias where people put too much weight on what happened most recently instead of looking at the full picture.

Once again this year the masses are falling victim to recency bias by assuming injuries from last season (which players have fully recovered from) are “guaranteed” to lead to injuries again this season.

**This psychological trap creates some of the best draft values every single year.** While your league mates are still haunted by last season's disappointments, you'll be scooping up players at massive discounts who are primed to bounce back. Christian McCaffrey is a perfect example from last season - he was fully healthy heading into the season yet many were scared to draft him due to his injury-riddled previous season.

# 5. Draft Strategies

*ADVANCED · 5m read · 2026*

Here is a breakdown of my personal favorite fantasy football draft strategies for this season that I will be using in leagues this year. **You never want to go into a draft locked into any specific strategy.** Understanding each strategy will allow you to react and optimize for the best strategy for you based on how the draft is unfolding.

## Robust RB Strategy

**Core Approach:** Draft three running backs within the first five rounds to secure workhorse backs for RB1, RB2, and flex positions. Then don’t draft any other RBs until round 10+.

**Strategic Advantage:** Capitalizes on scarcity of true workhorses in today’s NFL, creating a major positional advantage with weekly upside nearly impossible to replicate through later picks. Secure the scarcest commodity early, then exploit exceptional depth at wide receiver, quarterback, and tight end in rounds 6-10. This strategy also leaves room on your roster to take two tight ends and two quarterbacks if you go with a late-round approach for those positions.

**Risk vs. Reward:** There is higher injury risk with early running backs that has the potential to tank your season, but the potential for overwhelming weekly advantage makes this calculated risk worthwhile for maximum upside.

## Hero RB Strategy

**Core Approach:** Draft one or two running backs within the first five rounds to secure workhorse backs RB1, RB2. Then, don’t draft any other RBs until you fill out the rest of your starting lineup.

**Strategic Advantage:** This is a more balanced approach that captures most of the upside in the Robust RB Strategy without the significant risk that comes with the Zero RB Strategy.

**Risk vs. Reward:** The upside here, especially if you only take one RB with your first five picks, is that you then only need to hit on one late-round RB to have a dominant starting lineup, rather than needing two with the Zero-RB approach. The risk here is you are one injury away from being in serious trouble at the RB position. Grabbing the backup (handcuff) running back for your first RB selection with this strategy is highly recommended.

## Zero RB Strategy

**Core Approach:** Pass on running backs in the first 4-7 rounds, instead stockpiling elite wide receivers, securing a dominant tight end, and potentially locking up a top-tier quarterback. Then using most of your round 8+ picks on high-upside RBs who have:

- A potential path to 50-plus receptions
- Attachment to good offense for touchdown potential
- One injury away from workhorse role

**Strategic Advantage:** Elite wide receivers tend to be more consistent and durable throughout the season, while running backs face the highest injury risk of any fantasy position with very few true workhorses. With this approach your team can become unbeatable if you are able to hit on two late-round running backs or find startable running backs off waivers if there are a lot of injuries to early-round running backs throughout the season.

**Risk vs. Reward:** This is the highest risk and highest upside draft strategy that exists, which makes it perfect for highly competitive leagues with 14+ teams and in PPR leagues, where you can start more wide receivers in your lineup than running backs.

## Elite Tight End or PUNT

**Core Approach:** Draft **Brock Bowers, Trey McBride, or Colston Loveland**, with one of your first four picks.

**Strategic Advantage:** There is a STEEP drop off at the tight end position, so snagging one of the elite producers will give you a strong positional advantage at one of the weakest position groups. If you get one of these three, you don’t need to waste another pick the whole draft on a tight end. If you don’t get one of these three, this strategy has you waiting until round 9+ to take two high upside tight ends, rather than reaching in rounds 4-8 on a tight end with similar risk and upside to the players available in the later rounds.

**Risk vs. Reward:** This approach maximizes your upside and limits your risk. The worst case scenario when it comes to tight end drafting is spending an early or mid-round pick on a tight end that flops or performs worse than tight ends taken later in your draft.

## The Patient QB Approach

**Core Approach:** Don’t draft any quarterbacks in rounds 1 through 7 unless a top tier option slides 1+ round past their average draft position, otherwise opting to take two high ceiling options in round 8+.

**Strategic Advantage:** There are many quarterbacks with the potential to finish top-3 at the position this year who will be available later in drafts, freeing up your early picks on true difference makers at running back, wide receiver, and tight end. Taking two quarterbacks doubles your chances at hitting a ceiling outcome and the likelihood that one of your quarterbacks has a good matchup each week.

**Risk vs. Reward:** This approach maximizes your upside (finding a league-winning quarterback) and limits your risk because you are spending *less* draft capital on quarterback than the rest of your league. The worst case scenario is neither of your quarterbacks hit their ceiling and you are stuck searching the waiver wire all season for quarterback options (which is not necessarily as bad as it sounds).

## The Josh Allen Strategy

**Core Approach:** Draft **Josh Allen** in the 3rd round as the first quarterback taken in your draft.

**Strategic Advantage:** Josh Allen is in a tier of his own thanks to his elite weekly floor and ceiling that is hard to get from the rest of the quarterbacks. Allen has had at least 23 fantasy points per game for 6-consecutive seasons. If you get Allen, you don’t need to waste another pick on a quarterback, freeing up an additional bench spot for another position. This strategy also comes with weekly start/sit piece of mind at the quarterback position.

**Risk vs. Reward:** This approach locks in the clear top quarterback but comes at a significant cost passing up on potential league-winning receivers and running backs. The risk with this strategy is if Josh Allen fails to truly separate from a historically deep group of high-ceiling quarterback options in the middle rounds of drafts then you will have mostly wasted your 3rd round pick.

## Ignore Bye Weeks Strategy

**Core Approach:** Do not pay any attention to bye weeks while you are making draft decisions. This is an evergreen strategy not specific to this season.

**Strategic Advantage:** There are so many more important things to optimize for than avoiding overlapping bye weeks. Drafting the best team is what matters, if your team is good enough, you can afford risking 1 bye week related loss and there’s a waiver wire so bye weeks won’t guarantee a loss.

**Risk vs. Reward:** You may run into 1-2 stressful weeks with a lot of byes all at once.

If we zoom out and look at Kyler’s last 5 seasons, we see a consistent pattern with a larger sample size: he significantly boosts pass-catcher fantasy production when on the field. Across the board, DeAndre Hopkins, Marvin Harrison Jr., Trey McBride, Michael Wilson, Rondale Moore (RIP), and Greg Dortch all averaged an additional 5 points per game when Kyler played. Dortch and Rondale saw the biggest boosts, **which is a sign that the slot role for the Vikings this season could be extremely valuable for fantasy.** Jailen Nailor filled that role for the Vikings last season and is now on the Raiders, which means we will likely see all of the three Vikings receivers rotate more in the slot this season.

Kyler also increased his team’s running back target share by more than 2% when on the field over the last 5 seasons, which should help Aaron Jones who is expected to be the primary pass-catching running back for the Vikings this season. While he’s clearly had a big impact on passing production, the Cardinals running back rushing production over the last 5 years is nearly identical with vs. without Kyler on the field.

**Do not underrate the Vikings passing attack in fantasy football this season.**

## The Dolphins Blow It Up

**The Drastic Changes:** The Dolphins went from **Tua Tagovailoa** throwing to **Tyreek Hill and Jaylen Waddle** in **Mike McDaniel’s** offense to **Malik Willis** throwing to **Malik Washington, Chris Bell, Caleb Douglas, Jalen Tolbert, Tutu Atwell, and Greg Dulcich** with an entirely different coaching staff. **On paper, this is one of the worst wide receiver rooms in the history of the NFL.**

All signs point to the Dolphins wanting to be one of the most run-heavy teams in the NFL, despite the fact that they likely have one of the worst defenses and will be playing from behind often.

**Why it Matters for Fantasy:** Even with a historically bad pass-catching group, Malik Willis has top-5 fantasy QB upside with real potential to lead all QB’s in rushing and go over 1,000 rushing yards. He’s an ideal late-round QB target this season. This offense also appears poised to maximize the volume for **De’Von Achane**. He’s the only running back in the NFL with a real chance to lead his team in receiving. We’ve seen rushing quarterbacks improve the rushing efficiency of running backs in the past, which gives Achane a real chance to lead all running backs in yards per carry, a feat he has already achieved in two of his three NFL seasons playing alongside Tua. Achane is an excellent pick towards the end of the 1st round of fantasy drafts.

## The Chargers Level Up

**The Upgrades:** The Chargers enter 2026 with one of the most upgraded offenses in the NFL. **Rashawn Slater and Joe Alt** return healthy on the offensive line, and the team spent four additional draft picks reinforcing that unit. **Omarion Hampton** is back from injury with **Keaton Mitchell and Kimani Vidal** as complementary backs. **David Njoku** joins **Oronde Gadsden II** as a legitimate pass-catching tight end threat. Assuming **Justin Herbert** stays healthy, this offense will be elite.

On top of that, the Chargers hired **Mike McDaniel** as offensive coordinator. McDaniel’s scheme excels at creating complex, hard-to-defend concepts, which is exactly the kind of thing we need for top-tier fantasy production. Across his entire tenure as head coach of the Dolphins, Miami was 9th in scoring efficiency, 2nd in red zone passer rating, 8th in yards per game, 10th in points per game, 4th in completion percentage, and 8th in passing yards per game.

McDaniel’s offenses pepper the RB in targets, resulting in Achane finishing as the RB4 in receptions and receiving yards per game last season. Omarion Hampton and Keaton Mitchell have pass-catching skill sets, so there’s a natural fit there. Additionally, Justin Herbert has been among the highest in the NFL in checkdown passes since coming into the league. These two factors bode extremely well for the Chargers RBs.

Justin Herbert is currently going around QB10 on most platforms, and his ceiling is QB1. **Ladd McConkey** is also ranked ahead of his current ADP, and he has a ceiling of WR5. Omarion Hampton is ranked as a top-12 RB with a ceiling of RB4.

**The Chargers are set up for a massive offensive leap that could define fantasy football in 2026.**

## The Raiders Offensive Overhaul

**Last Year was BAD:** The upgrade from **Pete Carroll and Geno Smith** to **Klint Kubiak, Kirk Cousins, and/or Fernando Mendoza** cannot be understated. Smith finished outside the top-32 QBs in fantasy points per game, and averaged over 1 interception per game. This team is going from the oldest coach in the NFL to one of the best offensive coaches in the NFL that made **Sam Darnold** look elite last season.

**This Year Could be GOOD:** Kubiak brings one of the top schemes in the NFL to an offense that was largely inept in 2025. The Kubiak-led Seahawks offense ranked 1st in scoring offense, 3rd in total offense, 8th in passing offense, and 10th in rushing offense in 2025. In 2024 with the Saints, his offense ranked top five in EPA per play before injuries to the offensive line and Derek Carr derailed the season. His first time as an offensive coordinator led the Vikings to being 11th in passing offense, and 14th in total offense in 2021. Expected Week 1 starter, Kirk Cousins, has experience in Kubiak’s system, and the outlook for every weapon on this roster has improved significantly.

The Raiders also made substantial upgrades to their interior offensive line this offseason. They signed center **Tyler Linderbaum** to the highest contract for a center in NFL history, added veteran guard **Spencer Burford** on a one-year deal, and drafted guard **Trey Zuhn** in the 3rd round. All of this should help bring one of the league’s worst offensive lines from last year to at least league average this season.

Walter’s projections show value throughout the depth chart:

We have **Ashton Jeanty** projected to have a ceiling of RB3 after garnering the 2nd highest rush share among eligible RBs as a rookie. If he has a similar workload on this improved offense, Jeanty could win leagues. **Brock Bowers** is going to dominate the target share on this offense lacking WRs, which makes him a very intriguing pick in the 2nd or 3rd Rounds of fantasy drafts. Speaking of the WRs, one of **Tre Tucker, Jalen Nailor, or Jack Bech** are going to have fantasy value. Our money is on Jalen Nailor. He’s an underrated player, his 15.2 yards per reception ranked 10th highest in the NFL last season despite the fact that over half his snaps came from the slot and the Vikings had one of the worst QB situations in the league.

Jeanty can be a league-winner as a late 1st or early 2nd round pick. Brock Bowers has the target share potential to break every single-season tight end record this season. Jalen Nailor is our preferred target at wide receiver in Las Vegas and makes for a solid last round fantasy pick.

## The Green Bay Packers Consolidate the WR Room

**The Changes:** The Packers wide receivers have been a fantasy football start-sit headache the past several seasons due to constant rotation of 4-5 different options. The offseason departure of **Dontavion Wicks** (traded to Eagles) and **Romeo Doubs** (signed by Patriots) results in a much cleaner situation for the remaining Packers pass catchers (**Jayden Reed, Christian Watson, Mathew Golden, Tucker Kraft**).

**This Year Could be GREAT:** With more consistent target shares, all of the pass catchers in this offense should have much stronger weekly floors. If we combine these increased target shares with an increase in teaming pass volume, there will be league-winners from this passing offense. **Jordan Love** is one of the most efficient passers in the NFL, but his 29 passing attempts per game last season ranked 22nd among quarterbacks. This season we are projecting him for 32 attempts per game.

**Our projections have all four pass catchers ahead of ADP, with Christian Watson and Tucker Kraft being our favorite’s from the group.**

**TE · BAL**

He is in line for a larger role with Isaiah Likely now on the Giants yet his cost in drafts does not reflect that. Andrews should be a favorite target of **Lamar Jackson** and is available in round 9+ of most fantasy drafts. If you go with the late-round tight end strategy this season, Andrews is a great option with a top-10 TE ceiling to pair alongside a 2nd late-round tight end.

## Quarterbacks

### Round 1, 2, & 3

Quarterback may be the strongest it has ever been from a fantasy perspective. Our projections show 9 different quarterbacks have the potential to finish as the overall QB1 and nearly 20 different quarterbacks have a top-5 fantasy quarterback ceiling heading into this season. The depth at quarterback makes spending one of your first three picks on **Josh Allen** tough to justify in single quarterback leagues despite his elite floor and ceiling. If you are going to do it, getting Allen in round 3 would be ideal and make sure you don’t waste another pick on a 2nd quarterback later in your draft.

### Round 4 & 5

#### Lamar Jackson
**QB · BAL**

Through the first three games of last season, Jackson was averaging an elite 27.6 fantasy points per game before suffering a hamstring injury in Week 4. He then averaged just 15.3 points per game in his 7 full games after he returned from the injury. Jackson is statistically the greatest rushing quarterback of all time, we saw his floor outcome last season but we don’t want to forget his league-winning ceiling. Coming off a disappointing year, Lamar has the potential to slide into round 5+ in some drafts.

#### Drake Maye
**QB · NE**

**Maye** was the most efficient quarterback in the NFL last year, even when you adjust for strength of opponent. Now he enters year 3 with Romeo Doubs and A.J. Brown added to the roster. His elite floor and ceiling justify a 5th round pick this season.

### Round 6 & 7

#### Jayden Daniels
**QB · WAS**

**Daniels** had one of the greatest rookie fantasy seasons of all time in 2024, finishing with 22.4 fantasy points per game (QB4) and leading all quarterbacks with 54 rushing yards per game. He then played just 4 full games last season due to knee, hamstring, and elbow injuries. His ceiling makes him a high-value upside pick in this range of drafts, especially considering the potential boost from new offensive coordinator David Blough.

#### Jalen Hurts
**QB · PHI**

There are going to be drafts where **Jalen Hurts** falls into round 7+ because of the negative public sentiment surrounding him from last season and the departure of A.J. Brown. Yet, the tush-push remains a fantasy football cheat-code, especially in leagues with only 4 points per passing touchdown. Jalen Hurts has ranked top-10 in quarterback fantasy football points per game for 5-consecutive seasons, and he should benefit from having a new offensive coordinator after dealing with one of the worst coordinators in the NFL last year. And while the Eagles lost A.J. Brown, they did add Dontavion Wicks, Marquise Brown, and Eli Stowers to the roster this offseason who should provide some depth behind Devonta Smith, Saquon Barkley, and Dallas Goedert. Hurts’ floor ranks 2nd best among all quarterbacks in our projections. If you find yourself in a league full of Jalen Hurts haters, you could get one of the best values in the entire draft.

#### Caleb Williams
**QB · CHI**

Caleb jumped from 6.4 yards per attempt and a 3.6% touchdown rate in his rookie season to 7 yards per attempt and a 4.7% touchdown rate in his first season with head coach Ben Johnson. Our current projections are assuming similar efficiency to last season, and Williams still projects as a good value pick in round 7 of fantasy drafts. In year 2 of the Ben Johnson offense with Luther Burden and Colston Loveland also entering year 2, we should not be surprised if Caleb Williams efficiency takes another leap, giving him a ceiling of overall QB1.

#### Justin Herbert
**QB · LAC**

Herbert averaged over 22.5 fantasy points per game in each of his first two seasons in the NFL (2020 and 2021) but has been under 19 points per game each of the last 4 seasons. We’ve already seen a Mike McDaniels offense lead the NFL in passing yards with Tua, so it would not be a surprise to see Herbert achieve that this season. He has the 4th highest ceiling in our projections, making him one of the ideal mid-round QB targets.

### Round 8 & 9

These are my personal favorite quarterback value picks this year with high-ceiling that are available at a significant discount compared to other comparable quarterbacks.

#### Trevor Lawrence
**QB · JAX**

Following the Jakobi Meyers trade last season, Lawrence averaged an elite 25.7 fantasy points per game from Week 12 onwards. He finished the season with 8-consecutive games with at least 19 fantasy points. With one of the best wide receiver groups in the NFL, there is real overall QB1 upside here.

#### Brock Purdy
**QB · SF**

Purdy has quietly finished top-10 among quarterbacks in fantasy points per game in all 3 straight seasons. In his 8 full games (excluding Week 18) due to a shoulder and toe injury suffered early in the season he averaged 22.6 fantasy points per game last year, ranking 2nd best among quarterbacks behind only Josh Allen. With Mike Evans now on the team, the 49ers have arguably the best group of pass catchers in the NFL if George Kittle, CMC, and Deebo Samuel can stay healthy.

## Defenses and Kickers

There is no reason to draft a defense or kicker ahead of a skill position player, even if they finished with the most fantasy points last season. The **Texans D/ST and Brandon Aubrey** are currently being drafted around pick 135 in ESPN drafts, a range where **Patrick Mahomes, Justin Herbert, Isaiah Likely, Chris Rodriguez, and high upside rookies like Chris Bell, Omar Cooper Jr., and Germie Bernard** are all available. Do not be the first manager in your draft to take a defense or kicker. Draft defense and kicker last, then stream the best matchup-based options off the waiver wire throughout the season.

### Defense targets

Here are 3 defenses I will be targeting in the last round of drafts that should be good options to start the season and have potential to be worth holding onto for much of the season.

#### Los Angeles Chargers
**DEF · LAC**

The Chargers open against the Cardinals and Raiders in Weeks 1 and 2, two of the softest matchups in the league. Their defense is one of the most underrated units in the NFL, and Walter has them ranked as the DEF7 with a ceiling of DEF1. After those matchups, the Chargers face a difficult stretch heading into their Week 7 bye, taking on the Bills, Seahawks, Broncos, and Chiefs. Stream them with confidence in the first two weeks, then look elsewhere until Week 16 when they play the Dolphins.

#### Jacksonville Jaguars
**DEF · JAX**

The Jaguars open against the Browns, a team with bottom-tier quarterback play breaking in an entirely new offensive system. Walter projects Jacksonville as the DEF12 in the Rest-of-Season Rankings, despite a difficult season-long schedule. They are a strong Week 1 streaming option.

#### Detroit Lions
**DEF · DET**

Outside of Buffalo in Week 2, the Lions take on the Saints, Jets, Panthers, and Cardinals before their Week 6 bye. They have a much more daunting schedule after that, but they will be a formidable option early in the season in matchups with weak offenses.

### Kicker targets

The number one thing that makes a kicker good is volume, how often his offense puts him in position to kick. Accuracy matters for job security, a kicker who misses regularly doesn’t keep his job, but it’s volume that actually drives fantasy points. Playing in a dome helps too, and good weather in general is important, particularly late in the season. Elite leg strength is a bonus (think Brandon Aubrey or Cam Little) but not a necessity. And once the season starts, streaming kickers is the easiest edge in your league, because no one else bothers to do it. If you don’t have a top five kicker, you can stream a top five matchup every week, or grab the undrafted gems like Jason Myers.

Here are 3 kickers I will be targeting in the last round of drafts that have solid floors and a ton of upside.

#### Will Reichard
**K · MIN**

Reichard is attached to a Vikings offense that was mediocre in 2025 but should improve in 2026. He made the fifth most field goals in the league last year on the eighth most attempts and was one of the most accurate kickers in football, hitting 94.3% of his tries, third-best among qualified kickers. Pair that volume with that accuracy and a dome to kick in, and you’ve got a guy with top three upside. Reichard is Walter’s K6 with a ceiling of K1.

#### Charlie Smyth
**K · NO**

Smyth, a Northern Ireland native, took over the Saints’ job late in the year and immediately looked the part, averaging 10.0 fantasy points per game, tied for fifth-best at the position. New Orleans attempted the third most field goals in the league last season, and Smyth gets the benefit of playing half his games in the Caesars Superdome. He’s tied to an ascending offense that should keep creating opportunities. Smyth is Walter’s K4 with a ceiling of K1.

#### Eddy Pineiro
**K · SF**

“Steady” Eddy Pineiro doesn’t have flashy volume (it’s similar to Minnesota’s), but he’s in the most stable situation of the three. San Francisco’s offense is good, ranking top ten in both yards and points. Among kickers who started at least 14 games, Pineiro ranked fifth in points per game. Attach yourself to a good offense inside a tough division full of strong defenses, and there’s going to be a steady stream of high-leverage field goals. “Steady” Eddy makes them. Pineiro is Walter’s K7 with a ceiling of K1.

# 9. Do NOT Draft

*ADVANCED · 5m read · 2026*

These are 17 players I will NOT draft in fantasy football this season. Last year this list included guys like Isiah Pacheco, Joe Mixon, Chris Godwin, Cooper Kupp, Sam LaPorta, Travis Kelce, Evan Engram, and Jonnu Smith.

It also included guys like **Kyren Williams and James Cook** who had solid seasons, so it definitely won’t be a perfect list but it will help us avoid a LOT of busts in fantasy football.

## Running Backs

### 1. Josh Jacobs
**RB · GB**

**Jacobs** will be nearly 29 years old by the end of this season, and showed declining efficiency last season, particularly down the stretch as he battled through injuries. Jacobs ended last season with 3-straight games under 7 PPR fantasy points if we include the NFL playoffs, and he averaged just 8.7 points per game from Week 11-17. With **Marshawn Lloyd** looking fully healthy, it’s also likely we see a dip in usage and playing time for Jacobs this season. And I haven’t even mentioned the fact that Jacobs comes with some suspension risk due to ongoing legal issues. I would **MUCH** rather take the wide receivers going in this range of drafts, and prefer running backs like **Javonte Williams and Breece Hall** as well.

### 2. Quinshon Judkins
**RB · CLE**

**Todd Monken** has historically used a running back by committee approach, and while **Judkins** is a talented runner that likely means **Dylan Sampson** is the primary pass catcher in the backfield. Judkins averaged under 2 receptions per game last season, making him a TD dependent running back on an offense that could struggle to score points with one of the shakiest QB situations in the NFL.

### 3. Jadarian Price
**RB · SEA · Rookie**

What happens when **Zach Charbonnet** comes back for fantasy playoffs? I am also worried the Seahawks will be a committee without Charb. Also, there is no statistical evidence **Jadarian Price** can contribute as a pass-catcher. A touchdown-dependent committee back with a weaker playoff projection is a hard fade. TD-dependent committee back with a worse projection for the fantasy playoffs? Count me OUT.

### 4. Tony Pollard
**RB · TEN**

In the first four weeks of the last season, **Tony Pollard** was in a true workhorse role, playing 86% of snaps with Tyjae Spears sidelined due to injury. After Spears returned, his snapshare fell to 53% the rest of the season. Pollard was unstartable for most of last season, he did not have a single game over 15 PPR fantasy points until Week 14. He is as 29-year-old running back that will likely be in a committee again this season with **Tyjae Spears** and rookie running back **Nicholas Singleton**. This simply is not a league-winning running back profile, I’d much prefer to use my mid round picks on players with a higher ceiling such as Blake Corum.

### 5. Jacory Croskey-Merritt
**RB · WAS**

**JCM** has an extremely low probability of becoming a league-winning workhorse back. Last season, despite a season-ending injury for **Austin Ekeler** in Week 2, and **Chris Rodriguez** missing 4 games, JCM averaged just a 37% rush share and a 3% target share. He did not have a single game with more than 2 targets with the team opting to play **Jeremy McNichols** over him in the pass-catching role. Last year showed that even if there are injuries in this backfield, this WILL be a committee and Bill is fighting to be a part of it. He is a touchdown dependent former 7th-round NFL draft pick with no receiving upside that likely will be in a committee all season even if there are injuries to other running backs on the team. I much prefer the RBs going around him in drafts.

## Wide Receivers

### 6. Davante Adams
**WR · LAR**

**Davante Adams** is an obvious regression candidate in 2026. His 23.3% touchdown rate and 14 touchdowns in 14 regular season games in 2025 are simply not replicable. He’s still an elite talent, but I refuse to chase those absurd touchdown numbers. Since 2020 Adams has not had back-to-back seasons with a touchdown rate over 10%. I’m avoiding him at his lofty draft cost.

### 7. DJ Moore
**WR · BUF**

**D.J. Moore** finished as the WR43 in PPR points per game in 2025, which is a clear regression from his previous production. The Bills have lacked a true WR1 since Stefon Diggs’ departure, consistently spreading the ball around without rhyme or reason. **Joe Brady’s** offense offers no guarantee of target share consistency. His fifth-round draft cost is too steep.

### 8. Courtland Sutton
**WR · DEN**

**Courtland Sutton** had a ceiling season in 2025, finishing as the WR16 in PPR points per game (13.6) with eight games of 16 or more fantasy points. With **Jaylen Waddle** now in the fold, a significant chunk of those targets disappears. New play-caller **Davis Webb** is also an unknown variable here. His offense may not suit Sutton’s game the way **Sean Payton’s** did. The round 7-8 range offers better value elsewhere.

### 9. DK Metcalf
**WR · PIT**

**DK Metcalf** is going several rounds before **Michael Pittman Jr.** on almost every platform. This gap is difficult to justify when our projections have Pittman slightly ahead in PPR formats. In 2025, Metcalf had only four games with 16 or more PPR points. You are better off waiting a couple rounds and taking Pittman and using the round 7-8 pick on a higher upside receiver, quarterback, or tight end than selecting Metcalf this year.

### 10. Michael Wilson
**WR · ARI**

The Cardinals led the NFL in pass attempts last season, it’s extremely unlikely that is the case again this season. A new coaching staff and the third overall pick spent on running back **Jeremiyah Love** signal a clear shift in offensive identity. **Michael Wilson’s** breakout context from last season is also being overlooked.

Wilson averaged fewer than four points per game from Weeks 1 through 5 with Kyler Murray starting and a healthy Marvin Harrison Jr. on the field. He then averaged over 16 points per game the rest of the season with Jacoby Brissett starting. He finished as the WR11 during that stretch. Harrison had over 60% route participation in just three games during that same window due to injury.

I’d prefer not to chase last year’s production with Wilson.

### 11. Travis Hunter
**WR · JAX**

There’s just no way **Travis Hunter** will be a consistent starter you can trust unless there are multiple injuries to the Jaguars wide receiver room. He’s going as a top-100 pick right now on ESPN and top-150 on most platforms; there are much higher ceiling options available this late in drafts.

### 12. Romeo Doubs
**WR · NE**

Doubs signed with the Patriots this offseason, but he will be a start/sit nightmare week-to-week competing for targets with **Kayshon Boutte, Demario Douglas, Mack Hollins, and Hunter Henry** behind A.J. Brown. Doubs will likely be in a role similar to **Stefon Diggs** last year where he is playing only around half the snaps. Don’t waste a later round pick on him.

### 13. Brandon Aiyuk
**WR · SF**

While not being overly sought after in drafts, **Brandon Aiyuk’s** situation is giving very similar vibes to **Joe Mixon** from last season. The two situations are very different, but the outcome is looking increasingly likely to be the same. There’s a very real chance that Aiyuk doesn’t play a single snap this season. And if he does, are we sure Aiyuk is still a talented NFL WR?

## Tight Ends

### 14. Tyler Warren
**TE · IND**

While talented, **Tyler Warren** is too costly in drafts, especially when factoring in how closely he’s being taken to **Colston Loveland**. Warren doesn’t have the ceiling of Loveland, and our projections much prefer him in rounds 3-5 of drafts than Tyler Warren. **Daniel Jones** is coming back from a torn achilles, which brings more risk to the entire Colts passing attack. Warren’s draft cost is simply too high for this level of risk early in drafts.

### 15. Harold Fannin Jr.
**TE · CLE**

**Harold Fannin Jr.** projects as a negative regression candidate in 2026. Subpar quarterback play and a new play-caller make a repeat of his rookie season an uphill battle. With Fannin going in the first 90 picks of most drafts, I’d rather take one of the big three TE options or wait a bit longer and take tight ends like **Tucker Kraft, George Kittle, Sam LaPorta, or Isaiah Likely** who have comparable (or better) ceilings later in drafts.

## Quarterbacks

### 16. Matthew Stafford
**QB · LAR**

**Matthew Stafford** is coming off an MVP season with a ridiculously high 7.5% touchdown rate. Since 2020 Stafford has not had back-to-back seasons with a touchdown rate over 5%. Stafford has an inflated draft cost this year despite the likely negative regression that will hit him this season in the touchdown category. I’d much rather wait and take a quarterback with rushing upside later in my draft than waste a mid-round pick that is very unlikely to sustain league-leading touchdown efficiency again this season.

### 17. Cameron Ward
**QB · TEN**

Nobody is actively looking to draft **Cam Ward** in redraft leagues, but I want to acknowledge just how terrible Ward was statistically as a rookie. He was 42nd in fantasy points per game among QBs, 34th in rushing yards per game, and 35th in passing TD rate. It is truly hard to be any worse than this statistically. They added some exciting weapons, and a much better play-caller in **Brian Daboll**, but I’d much rather take the quarterbacks available a few rounds earlier like **Malik Willis, Kyler Murray, and Tyler Shough** than needing to rely on the worst statistical quarterback from last season.

# 10. Sleepers

*ADVANCED · 3m read · 2026*

When drafting sleepers in the late rounds generally the best strategy is to target players that are being underrated due to circumstances from last season that no longer exist and/or players that could disproportionately benefit from an injury to the starter ahead of them on the depth chart. These are the types of sleepers that can turn into league winners.

## Sleeper Targets

These are players that are unlikely to get drafted in your initial starting lineup who have underrated league-winning potential in the mid to late rounds of fantasy drafts.

### Wide Receivers

#### Xavier Worthy
**WR · KC**

The argument for Worthy as a sleeper this season is fairly straight forward. He suffered a shoulder injury 3 snaps into Week 1 last season, and he played through the injury the rest of a disappointing season. He has one of the best quarterbacks in the league in **Patrick Mahomes** and very little target competition outside of **Rashee Rice** and a 37-year-old **Travis Kelce**. In Worthy’s 10 career games playing at least 80% of snaps, he is averaging 15.2 fantasy points per game. The floor is low, but this type of ceiling is exactly what I want in the later rounds of fantasy drafts.

### Running Backs

#### Blake Corum
**RB · LAR**

Corum is a league-winning handcuff if Kyren Williams misses time and he should offer a usable floor even without a Kyren injury. Off-season reporting from The Athletic suggests this backfield will be closer to a 50-50 split than current ADP suggests. Last year after the Rams Week 8 bye Corum regularly alternated drives and was playing ~40% of snaps compared to the 25% snap share he had before the bye.

#### Jonathon Brooks
**RB · CAR**

Brooks enters 2026 healthy with a clear path to becoming the Panther’s lead back. Carolina let **Rico Dowdle** walk for a reason. Brooks has a very high ceiling if he stays healthy, and he’s going too late in drafts.

#### Marshawn Lloyd
**RB · GB**

Plagued by injuries, Lloyd has just 1 career game played through his first 2 NFL seasons in which he dealt with hamstring and ankle strains in addition to appendicitis. However, all signs this offseason point to Lloyd being fully healthy and in the clear lead for the Packers RB2 role behind **Josh Jacobs**. Jacobs is still at risk of a suspension due to ongoing legal issues, and is coming off an inefficient injury-riddled season at 28 years old. Even without Jacobs missing time, Lloyd should offer some standalone value. He should have an elite ceiling in any games without Jacobs playing, making him a strong late-round dart throw in drafts.

### Tight Ends

#### Isaiah Likely
**TE · NYG**

If I miss out on my core tight end targets, then I am happy to wait until round 10+ to take Isaiah Likely, who will be in an expanded role this year on the Giants. Outside of **Malik Nabers**, the Giants have plenty of target opportunity, and Likely has ranked top-15 in yards per target and yards per reception for 3-straight seasons. The problem in the past is he has been playing only about 40-50% of snaps while playing alongside **Mark Andrews**. Likely has top-5 TE upside in an expanded role and he is being picked outside the top-12 TE’s.

### Quarterbacks

#### Malik Willis
**QB · MIA**

Willis joins Miami after stints in Tennessee and Green Bay. Willis brings an elite rushing skill set that gives him a high weekly ceiling independent of his supporting cast. He scored 25 fantasy points against the Titans in 2024 and 31.5 against the Ravens in 2025, showcasing his weekly ceiling capability. The Dolphins project to trail in most games, creating favorable game scripts for Willis throughout the season. If he doesn’t work out, we can simply drop him and work the waivers. You simply should not be able to get a quarterback capable of rushing for over 1,000 yards this late in fantasy drafts. He is an ideal target if you go with a late-round QB strategy.

#### Tyler Shough
**QB · NO**

Last year as a rookie, Shough averaged 17.6 fantasy points per game across his 9 full games. He finished the season strong with six straight games over 17 fantasy points. We often see quarterbacks take a step forward in year 2, and he should benefit from the Saints fast-paced offense and the addition of talented rookie wide receiver **Jordyn Tyson**. Our projections have Shough ranked ahead of his ADP on every platform.

#### Kyler Murray
**QB · MIN**

There is no hiding the fact that Kyler’s last season on the Cardinals was underwhelming. He averaged just 16.2 fantasy points per game without a single game over 18.5 points before getting benched for **Jacoby Brissett** in Week 6 (who was more efficient than him as starter). At the same time, we can’t ignore the long history of solid fantasy production from Kyler Murray, prior to last season he ranked top-12 in quarterback fantasy points per game in seven straight seasons (every other year since he entered the NFL). Now, he finds himself in a much better offensive scheme with a great group of receivers. He’s a great late round quarterback to target in drafts.

## Deep Sleeper Targets

These are players likely to be available in the last round of most fantasy drafts that have real upside and a good chance of being waiver wire targets early in the season if they go undrafted.

### Sean Tucker
**RB · TB**

All signs point to a significant role for Sean Tucker this season, and based on his current draft cost, most fantasy drafters do not seem to care. Tucker is poised to get the majority of goal-line work for the Buccaneers this season while sharing a backfield with **Bucky Irving and Kenneth Gainwell**. Tucker led the NFL in touchdown rate last season, and while that is likely to regress, he has plenty of room to see increased playing time coming off a season where he had just a 19% snap percentage. Tucker has averaged 18 PPR points per game in five games over a 30% snap percentage the last two seasons. He’s a high-ceiling last round pick, especially in half-PPR and non-PPR leagues.

### Ryan Flournoy
**WR · DAL**

In his six games where he played over 50% of the snaps last season, **Ryan Flournoy** averaged 13.4 PPR points per game. Flournoy would be an instant WR3 with WR2 upside in fantasy football if CeeDee Lamb or George Pickens miss time, and he has the potential for usable weeks in deeper leagues without any injuries.

### Zachariah Branch
**WR · ATL · Rookie**

**Zachariah Branch** was one of our favorite rookies in this class, and his skill set pairs well with Drake London’s. Branch is going outside the top-200 picks, but profiles as the after-the-catch, dynamic slot receiver Kevin Stefanski likes to deploy. He is worth your final pick.

### Jalen Nailor
**WR · LV**

**Jalen Nailor** was one of the most underrated offseason signings for the Raiders. Nailor has experience with Kirk Cousins and Klint Kubiak, and his 15.2 yards per reception ranked 10th highest in the NFL last season despite the fact that over half his snaps came from the slot and he had one of the worst QB situations in the league. He has a real path to the most WR targets in Las Vegas.

### Gunnar Helm
**TE · TEN**

Didn’t electrify as a rookie, but he had 6 games with 4 or more receptions, and 3 games with double-digit PPR points. His 1.6 yards per route run also ranked 18th best among tight ends last season, an impressive feat considering the Titans had the 3rd fewest passing yards in the NFL last season. **Chig Okonkwo** left in free agency, and the only additions Tennessee made at TE were **Daniel Bellinger** and 7th Round draft choice **Jaran Kanak**. Helm has a top-12 tight end ceiling in our projections, making him a good late-round target.

### Terrance Ferguson
**TE · LAR**

After a very modest rookie season, Ferguson projects as an intriguing sleeper on a high octane offense. Ferguson was a 2nd round pick in last year’s NFL draft, and he had just 3 games all season with at least 2 receptions. Small sample size, but in those 3 games he averaged 11 fantasy points per game. Year 2 and 3 are the biggest leaps we typically see from tight ends, if he is used in more of a full-time role his ceiling is a top-5 fantasy tight end. He’s free at the end of almost every draft, making him a great last round target if you go with a late-round tight strategy.

### Greg Dulcich
**TE · MIA**

It may seem far-fetched, **Greg Dulcich** is one of the only reliable targets on the Dolphins’ offense and is going undrafted in every league. He recorded three or more receptions in four of his final five games in 2025. If he becomes the top red zone target for **Malik Willis** (which is certainly possible), he would have a pathway to fantasy football excellence. He’s going undrafted in every league.

## Super Deep Sleepers

These are players going undrafted in all leagues that your leaguemates are not thinking about. Consider them in deeper leagues or as players to monitor on the waiver wire.

### Kaleb Johnson
**RB · PIT**

Hear me out on this one. I’m fully aware that **Kaleb Johnson** was horrendous as a rookie in his limited opportunities. But, he was a third round pick, and if Rico Dowdle or Jaylen Warren miss time, he would be thrust into a vital role in the Pittsburgh offense. Side note: He was one of my favorite prospects in his class, and I am still holding out hope.

### Michael Mayer
**TE · LV**

Mayer has been largely overshadowed by **Brock Bowers** over the last two seasons, making him one of the most overlooked tight end talents in the NFL. The Raiders got the biggest coaching staff upgrade of the offseason and currently have one of the weakest wide receiver depth charts of all time. All signs point to more two TE sets for the Raiders this season which should lead to usable fantasy weeks with Mayer. Kubiak offenses have ranked top-8 in 2-TE formation usage (also referred to as 12-personnel) each of the last two seasons. And if Bowers misses time with injury, Mayer has league-winning upside. He’s free in every draft, making him a solid pick whether you pick Bowers or not.

## Super Super Deep Sleepers

### Phil Mafah
**RB · DAL**

If Kaleb Johnson and Michael Mayer were not deep enough for you, let me introduce you to **Phil Mafah**, a player most of your leaguemates have never heard of. Mafah missed the majority of his rookie season due to a pre-season shoulder injury after getting selected by the Dallas Cowboys in the 7th round of the NFL draft. Despite the weak draft capital, Mafah was a strong college prospect out of Clemson who improved every collegiate season and tallied 42 receptions over his final two years. In his one NFL game played last season, he scored 10.9 PPR fantasy points on just 7 touches. He’s a darkhorse candidate to be the RB2 for the Cowboys, a role that would be extremely valuable if Javonte Williams misses any time.

### Tahj Brooks
**RB · CIN**

**Chase Brown** is the entrenched starter for the Bengals, but there is extremely little depth behind him. If Chase Brown were to miss time, Tahj Brooks would become a potential league-winner and offers a much higher ceiling than 31-year-old **Samaje Perine** (the other Bengals backup running back). Brooks was active for 16 games last year as a rookie but played more of a special teams role, and coming out of college he profiled as an all-purpose back capable of succeeding in a goal-line and pass-catching role. Brooks is an injury away from league-winning upside, making him a name to watch on waivers and a target in late rounds of deeper leagues.
